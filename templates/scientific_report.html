🔬 Scientific Honesty, Limitations & Quantum Hardware Roadmap

Transparent academic disclosure for Smart India Hackathon jury review

Peer-Reviewed Integrity
📜
Core Scientific Tenet of this Project:
We do NOT claim artificial "quantum superiority." Quantum computing is not magically faster for all problems. In current NISQ-era simulation, classical decision tree ensembles (Random Forest) run orders of magnitude faster and achieve comparable or superior F1-scores on tabular network flow data. Our prototype provides an honest, empirical comparison based on genuine algorithmic execution.

✓ 1. What is Actually Implemented

  • • Ingestion, cleaning (inf/NaN handling, deduplication) of authentic CIC-IDS2017 network traffic CSVs.
  • • Leakage-free preprocessing: Scalers, PCA, and MinMax feature transforms fitted strictly on train sets.
  • • Full-scale classical Random Forest baseline with real training and inference timing.
  • • Genuine PennyLane quantum circuits with angle encoding and CNOT entangling ladders.
  • • Quantum statevector computation and exact quantum kernel matrix evaluation.
  • • 3-party Bell-state quantum digital signature density matrix simulation with depolarizing Pauli noise.

📊 2. What is Experimentally Demonstrated

  • • Side-by-side accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and confusion matrices on identical train/test splits.
  • • Exponential simulation overhead: Classical microsecond latency vs quantum millisecond simulation latency.
  • • Compression of 78 network features down to 4 orthogonal quantum rotation angles via PCA.
  • • Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER) and state fidelity degradation under cyber threat attack vectors.

⚛️ 3. What is Simulated (Classical CPU Execution)

  • • The quantum model runs on PennyLane's default.qubit simulator on an x86 Intel/AMD CPU.
  • • Statevectors of length $2^N$ (16 complex amplitudes for 4 qubits) are stored in standard classical RAM.
  • • No real quantum computer was used for these runs, eliminating queue times and NISQ thermal decoherence noise.
  • • Simulation on classical CPUs provides zero physical quantum speedup.

🚀 4. Conceptual & Future Hardware Roadmap

  • • Execution on physical cloud QPUs (e.g. IBM Quantum Eagle/Heron or IonQ Forte) via Qiskit Runtime.
  • • Error mitigation techniques (Zero-Noise Extrapolation, Readout Error Mitigation) for physical quantum hardware.
  • • Fiber-optic Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) links transmitting entangled Bell photon pairs for hardware QDS.
  • • Quantum RAM (QRAM) architectures for $O(\log N)$ state preparation of large cybersecurity databases.

⚠️ Dataset-Level Intrusion vs Digital Signature Threat Mapping

The Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity (CIC-IDS2017) dataset contains network-flow records (packet counts, durations, flags, inter-arrival times) with attack labels such as Web Attack – Brute Force, Web Attack – Sql Injection, Web Attack – XSS, and PortScan.

The dataset does NOT contain cryptographic digital signature bytes directly. In modern enterprise IT, digital signature authorities (PKI signing servers, HSMs, OCSP responders) operate as network services. Our prototype establishes a technically sound application-layer bridge: network intrusions targeting signing APIs are classified, and their estimated threat probability is fed into the quantum digital signature verification logic to simulate defense against unauthorized impersonation and key tampering.
