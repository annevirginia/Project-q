🔬 Scientific Honesty & Hardware Roadmap

Transparent research limits, classical superiority on tabular data, and QPU roadmap

Academic Honesty
Key Scientific Takeaway: Quantum Computing is NOT magic. On current classical computers, simulating an N-qubit quantum state requires tracking 2N complex statevector amplitudes. For tabular network telemetry without intrinsic quantum mechanical symmetry, classical ensembles like Random Forest remain superior in speed and accuracy.

Simulator Limitations on Classical Hardware

  • Exponential Memory Scaling: Simulating 78 qubits would require 278 complex numbers (~4.7 million yottabytes), requiring dimensional reduction to 4 qubits.
  • Gram Matrix Bottleneck: Computing quantum state overlap K(x, x') for N samples requires O(N2) quantum circuit runs.
  • Lack of Quantum Speedup: On classical x86 CPUs, quantum simulation is slower than classical matrix algebra. True speedup requires physical QPUs.

Hardware Roadmap to True Quantum Advantage

  • Phase 1 (Present Prototype): 4-8 Qubit simulation on classical CPU via PennyLane / Qiskit with authentic CIC flow data.
  • Phase 2 (NISQ Hardware): Execution on IBM Quantum / Rigetti superconducting QPUs (32-127 qubits) with error mitigation.
  • Phase 3 (Fault-Tolerant Quantum PKI): Logical error-corrected qubits running physical Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) and multi-party Gottesman-Chuang QDS protocols.
